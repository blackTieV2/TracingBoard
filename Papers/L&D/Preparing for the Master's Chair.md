# Preparing for the Master’s Chair

_A Learning and Development Module_

Masonic Lodges are largely autonomous organisations operating within the broad structure of their respective Districts under the Constitutions of the United Grand Lodge of England. Each Lodge is, therefore, special and unique with its own set of Regulations, customs, traditions and practices. 

It will be apparent immediately, therefore, that any Learning and Development guidance must, of necessity, be broad-based but capable of adaptation to suit the needs of each individual Lodge and member.

This module contains guidance which, if properly applied, is intended to serve the needs of every Brother who has aspirations to become Master of his Lodge. In this sense, it represents an idealised picture of how a Lodge should operate, but this should not detract from its usefulness.

It is for each member to consider each part of the module and then to apply the advice as closely as possible within his own Lodge, bearing in mind the aforementioned customs and traditions.

## The Module is in two Parts:
1. PREPARATION  
2. OCCUPYING THE CHAIR

---

# PART ONE: PREPARATION

Preparation takes time. If you speak to an experienced Mason of long standing who has been Master of his Lodge he will probably tell you that it took over a decade, at least to reach that rank following his initiation. That meant that there was plenty of time to “learn the ropes.”

These days, largely because of the reduction in membership, there is more pressure, and less time, to progress through the ranks. Preparation, even in the form of occasional reflection and thought, should begin as early as possible.

Every Brother who wishes to attain the Master’s Chair in his Lodge should be encouraged to do so, as it is the highest honour that any Lodge can bestow. Naturally, then, it follows that if someone is fortunate enough to be elected to this position, he will want to have a successful year in office which he can look back on with pleasure and satisfaction.

The key to success lies in proper preparation. Ideally, this should develop over several years, reaching its most intense period when occupying the Junior Warden’s chair with a view to progressing, naturally, through the Senior Warden’s chair but this, of course, is not always possible.

---

### Attending Management Committees Before Your Year

- This is an important part of preparation; here, you will discover what happens behind the scenes in your Lodge and/or meeting place.
- Carefully observe everything said and done, identifying members’ talents, strengths and weaknesses as well as their desire (or otherwise) for involvement and advancement.
- Accept invitations to attend the interviews for candidates, formal or informal.
- As Master Elect, you will usually be expected to present and propose your own officers in advance of your year in the Chair.

---

### Selecting and Working with Officers

- Remember, you may only get one shot at this during your Masonic life, better try to get it right!
- Make yourself acquainted with the Book of Constitutions, especially Rule 104, so that you are fully aware of the differences between Principal Officers, Regular Officers and others. Identify those who take precedence.
- If you’re unsure, take advice from those with appropriate experience.
- It will be your responsibility to demonstrate leadership in the smooth running of the Lodge, to know its strengths and weaknesses, and to select the right people with the right attributes for the benefit of the Lodge and the enjoyment of the members.

---

### Selecting Non-Progressive Officers

- Remember, you do not automatically inherit the situation from your predecessor.
- Think carefully, with sensitivity, about senior non-progressive officers such as the Secretary and Director of Ceremonies and do not simply re-appoint because there is no obvious successor.

---

### Selecting Progressive Officers

- Think carefully again about those officers from Inner Guard to Senior Warden.
- Assist Brethren to meet their own aspirations.
- Investigate and offer helpful advice, support and encouragement to reluctant members.
- Be prepared to tackle issues of confidence and competence.
- Consult, but do not be afraid to replace a failing Officer.

---

### Overseeing Day-to-Day Matters Concerning the Lodge

- Understand the Lodge finances by consulting the Treasurer.
- Ensure prompt Annual and Installation Returns via the Secretary.
- Learn about District and National Charities.
- Start to engage with the Lodge Membership Team. If your Lodge does not have a team, then consider if convening a team might be an option during your year in the chair.
- Support the Membership Officer regarding recruitment and retention.
- Discuss with the Almoner his case load.
- Encourage and support the Charity Steward.
- Identify a possible requirement for a Lodge of Instruction or similar.
- Support all those representing your Lodge on the Building/Hall Management Committee and on District Committees.
- Keep an eye out for special anniversaries – Lodge or individual.
- Engage with social activities and Festivals, Open Days, Gavel Nights, White Table events etc.
- Engage with Remembrance Day Parades.
- Establish good relations with other Lodges through visiting.
- Identify opportunities to get involved with local community activities.

---

### Planning the Year’s Work – with Secretary, Director of Ceremonies, Treasurer etc.

- Plan as much as you can in advance to cover:
    - Ceremonies – candidates and their status/progression.
    - Presentation of Accounts.
    - Elections.
    - Official District Visits, VSO visits, Fraternal Visits.
    - Bank holidays and possible requirements to seek Dispensations.
    - No ceremonies? Consider alternatives e.g. lecture, demonstration, discussion.

---

### Interviewing and Balloting Candidates

- Keep interviews relaxed and friendly.
- Familiarise yourself with the essential questions.
- Ensure the interview is beneficial not only to the Lodge but to the Candidate to ensure a “good fit”.
- Explain each step in the procedure to the Candidate.
- If possible, try not to hold the ballot immediately prior to the Initiation ceremony.
- Leave at least one clear meeting between each stage of a Candidate’s advancement in order for him to settle, learn and reflect properly.

> **Remember:** When the candidate is balloted for and initiated on the same evening, or advanced from one degree to another extremely quickly, it is usually because the Lodge sees a need to have a ceremony. The progress of a Candidate should not be governed by the needs of a Lodge that wants something to do, but rather should at all times be geared to the needs of the Candidate.

## Key Duties of Important Offices

### The Secretary

- Strive to create an effective, mutually supportive, working relationship with the Secretary.
- To work effectively with you, your Secretary should:
  - Consult you on items for agenda of committee meetings and allow you to control such meetings.
  - Keep you informed of correspondence received and forward invitations promptly.
  - Discuss matters pertaining to District Grand Lodge before a reply is sent.
  - Complete and return all required Annual Returns in consultation with you.
  - Alert you immediately if any problem arises.

### The Director of Ceremonies

- Responsible for all matters of ritual and procedure.
- To ensure that you get the best possible support from your DC, he should undertake the following (may delegate but it nevertheless remains his responsibility):
  - Ensure that all the Officers understand that they must be present at meetings.
  - Arrange deputies in cases where it is impossible for an officer to attend.
  - Ensure Candidates for Passing and Raising know their answers.
  - Check the Lodge layout before each meeting.
  - Finalise details of the Festive Board (wine taking, toasts etc.) with you beforehand.
  - Ensure that JW understands his role as Lodge Steward to ensure that visitors are catered for and properly looked after.
  - Take a keen interest in the progress being made in the Lodge of Instruction.

## The Almoner

The Almoner is responsible for ensuring that the Lodge meets its commitments to relieve poverty and distress among its members and their families. Key duties include:

- Liaising with Masonic charities and preparing petitions.
- Keeping the Lodge informed of issues and coordinating responses.
- Promptly reporting all cases of distress to the Master.
- Representing the Master to the wives and dependants of Lodge members.
- Investigating and recommending solutions for financial difficulties.
- Maintaining detailed knowledge of the petition process.
- Being energetic, sensitive, discreet, and able to deal with strangers while maintaining confidentiality.
- Having thorough understanding of Masonic charities and the local support environment.
- Being mobile and dedicating ample time for visiting distressed members or dependants.
- This role benefits from continuity and often requires a commitment of around five years.

The Almoner thus acts as the "eyes and ears" of the Lodge, maintaining regular contact with sick or distressed members, widows, dependants, and those who have resigned. This includes making visits, listening actively, and ensuring those in need receive appropriate support [web:3][web:5][web:6].

## The Charity Steward

The Charity Steward's key objective is to ensure that the Lodge enthusiastically and wholeheartedly supports its charitable initiatives. Responsibilities include:

- Sending details of the year's charitable focus out to all members early.
- Encouraging the use of Gift Aid and promptly responding to requests for Gift Aid forms.
- Chasing up members slow to contribute.
- Keeping the Master informed of the progress of charitable giving.
- Advising the Master promptly on correspondence received from Masonic charities.
- Offering ideas and thoughts on additional fundraising activities.

The Charity Steward plays a crucial role in motivating members and fostering generosity toward charitable causes [original text].

---

## Lodge Mentor

The Lodge Mentor is tasked with providing a high level of personal support to ensure every member:

- Can enjoy being a Freemason.
- Understands the principles of the Craft.
- Becomes fully involved in Lodge activities.

The Mentor coordinates this whole-Lodge responsibility by:

- Ensuring a Mentor is appointed for every Candidate.
- Being fully aware of the mentoring process and what success looks like.
- Making all Lodge members aware of mentoring benefits.
- Ensuring Personal Mentors understand their aims and attend relevant training.
- Matching the right Mentor to the Candidate and nurturing a good initial relationship.
- Completing detailed mentoring reports and communicating these to the District Mentor.
- Reporting findings in Open Lodge.

This role is fundamental in member development and retention [original text].

---

## Lodge Membership Officer

The Lodge Membership Officer is a relatively recent but critical role charged to:

- Work closely with the Lodge Mentor and Almoner to create and maintain a membership group.
- Ensure members are fully aware of the Members’ Pathway and the Lodge’s engagement in promoting it.
- Prioritize recruitment and retention of members at all times.
- Keep abreast of all District initiatives regarding membership issues and advise the Master accordingly.
- Encourage Officers to work together harmoniously with enthusiasm and a sense of fun.

This role focuses on maintaining a vibrant and growing Lodge by ensuring member engagement is continuously nurtured.

# PART TWO: OCCUPYING THE CHAIR

## There are three main sections in this part of the guidance
- Occupying the Chair for Regular meetings.
- Occupying the Chair for Installation Meetings
- Occupying the Chair at Festive Boards.

### Occupying the Chair for Regular Meetings

Apart from the ceremony itself, a typical regular meeting generally includes these components (though not all may apply in every Lodge):

- **Summons**  
  Preparation and publication require close liaison with the Secretary. The Master should ensure having a personal working agenda at hand during the meeting, complete with notes and reminders.

- **Processing into the Lodge**  
  This involves welcoming visitors, Visitors’ Grand Officers (VGO), and fraternal visits from other Lodges, noting that not all Lodges practice this.

- **Eulogy**  
  This is to take place before other agenda items, as a mark of respect.

- **Minutes**  
  The minutes of the previous meeting are proposed, seconded, voted on, and approved by the Brethren, then confirmed by the Master's signature.

- **Salutations**  
  Salutations for Senior Brethren may be conducted as part of the meeting; however, not every Lodge includes this.

- **Ballots**  
  These should always be clearly explained to the Brethren regarding their purpose and the process to maintain transparency and fairness.

- **Election of Officers**  
  Typically includes election of the Worshipful Master, Treasurer, and Tyler, among others.

- **Reports**  
  Delivered by the Treasurer, Charity Steward, Almoner, and sometimes the Mentor or Membership Officer to keep the Lodge informed on finance, charity progress, welfare matters, and membership status.

- **Alms Collection**  
  Collections are held and should be clearly understood by the Brethren, emphasizing the purpose and use of collected funds.

- **Risings**  
  These are formal communications during the meeting communicated as three rises:  
  1st Rising – Grand Lodge  
  2nd Rising – District Grand Lodge  
  3rd Rising – Your Lodge

The Worshipful Master’s role during these meetings is to maintain order, oversee the smooth running of the agenda, and ensure that all ceremonial and administrative functions are conducted properly. Close coordination with the Secretary and Director of Ceremonies is essential to ensure familiarity with the agenda, participants, and any special arrangements. The Master should be prepared with notes and reminders to confidently guide the meeting proceedings [original document][web:16].
