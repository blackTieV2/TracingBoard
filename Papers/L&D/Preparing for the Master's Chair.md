# Preparing for the Master’s Chair


---

## Preparing for the Master’s Chair

*A Learning and Development Module for DGLEA Brethren*

Masonic Lodges are largely autonomous organisations operating within the broad structure of their respective **Districts** under the *Constitutions of the United Grand Lodge of England*.

Each Lodge is therefore special and unique, with its own set of regulations, customs, traditions, and practices.

It will be apparent immediately, therefore, that any Learning and Development guidance must, of necessity, be broad-based but capable of adaptation to suit the needs of each individual Lodge and member.

This module contains guidance which, if properly applied, is intended to serve the needs of every Brother who has aspirations to become Master of his Lodge. In this sense, it represents an idealised picture of how a Lodge should operate — but this should not detract from its usefulness.

It is for each member to consider each part of the module and then to apply the advice as closely as possible within his own Lodge, bearing in mind the aforementioned customs and traditions.

## PART ONE: PREPARATION

Preparation takes time. If you speak to an experienced Mason of long standing who has been Master of his Lodge, he will probably tell you that it took over a decade, at least, to reach that rank following his initiation. That meant that there was plenty of time to *learn the ropes.*

These days, largely because of the reduction in membership, there is more pressure — and less time — to progress through the ranks. Preparation, even in the form of occasional reflection and thought, should begin as early as possible.

Every Brother who wishes to attain the Master’s Chair in his Lodge should be encouraged to do so, as it is the highest honour that any Lodge can bestow. Naturally, then, it follows that if someone is fortunate enough to be elected to this position, he will want to have a successful year in office which he can look back on with pleasure and satisfaction.

The key to success lies in **proper preparation.** Ideally, this should develop over several years, reaching its most intense period when occupying the Junior Warden’s Chair, with a view to progressing naturally through the Senior Warden’s Chair — but this, of course, is not always possible.

---

### Attending Management Committees Before Your Year

* This is an important part of preparation; here, you will discover what happens behind the scenes in your Lodge and/or meeting place.
* Carefully observe everything said and done, identifying members’ talents, strengths and weaknesses, as well as their desire (or otherwise) for involvement and advancement.
* Accept invitations to attend the interviews for candidates, formal or informal.
* As Master Elect, you will usually be expected to present and propose your own officers in advance of your year in the Chair.

---

### Selecting and Working with Officers

* Remember, you may only get **one shot** at this during your Masonic life — better try to get it right!
* Make yourself acquainted with the *Book of Constitutions*, especially Rule 104, so that you are fully aware of the differences between Principal Officers, Regular Officers and others. Identify those who take precedence.
* If you’re unsure, take advice from those with appropriate experience.
* It will be your responsibility to demonstrate leadership in the smooth running of the Lodge, to know its strengths and weaknesses, and to select the right people with the right attributes for the benefit of the Lodge and the enjoyment of the members.

---

### Selecting Non-Progressive Officers

* Remember, you do not automatically inherit the situation from your predecessor.
* Think carefully, with sensitivity, about senior non-progressive officers such as the Secretary and Director of Ceremonies, and do not simply re-appoint because there is no obvious successor.

---

### Selecting Progressive Officers

* Think carefully again about those officers from Inner Guard to Senior Warden.
* Assist Brethren to meet their own aspirations.
* Investigate and offer helpful advice, support and encouragement to reluctant members.
* Be prepared to tackle issues of confidence and competence.
* Consult, but do not be afraid to replace a failing Officer.

---

### Overseeing the Day-to-Day Matters Concerning the Lodge

* Understand the Lodge finances by consulting the Treasurer.
* Ensure prompt Annual and Installation Returns via the Secretary.
* Learn about District and National Charities.
* Start to engage with the Lodge Membership Team. If your Lodge does not have a team, then consider if convening a team might be an option during your year in the Chair.
* Support the Membership Officer regarding recruitment and retention.
* Discuss with the Almoner his case load.
* Encourage and support the Charity Steward.
* Identify a possible requirement for a Lodge of Instruction or similar.
* Support all those representing your Lodge on the Building/Hall Management Committee and on District Committees.
* Keep an eye out for special anniversaries — Lodge or individual.
* Engage with social activities and Festivals, Open Days, Gavel Nights, White Table events, etc.
* Engage with Remembrance Day Parades.
* Establish good relations with other Lodges through visiting.
* Identify opportunities to get involved with local community activities.

---

### Planning the Year’s Work

*(With the Secretary, Director of Ceremonies, Treasurer, etc.)*

* Plan as much as you can in advance to cover:

  * Ceremonies – candidates and their status/progression.
  * Presentation of Accounts.
  * Elections.
  * Official District Visits, VSO visits, Fraternal Visits.
  * Bank holidays and possible requirements to seek Dispensations.
  * No ceremonies? Consider alternatives — e.g., lecture, demonstration, or discussion.

---

### Interviewing and Balloting Candidates

* Keep interviews relaxed and friendly.
* Familiarise yourself with the essential questions.
* Ensure the interview is beneficial not only to the Lodge but also to the Candidate, ensuring a “good fit.”
* Explain each step in the procedure to the Candidate.
* If possible, try not to hold the ballot immediately prior to the Initiation ceremony.
* Leave at least one clear meeting between each stage of a Candidate’s advancement to allow him to settle, learn, and reflect properly.

> **Remember:** When the candidate is balloted for and initiated on the same evening, or advanced from one degree to another extremely quickly, it is usually because the Lodge sees a need to have a ceremony.
> The progress of a Candidate should **not** be governed by the needs of a Lodge that wants something to do, but rather should at all times be geared to the needs of the Candidate.

### Key Duties of Important Offices

#### The Secretary

* Strive to create an effective, mutually supportive working relationship with the Secretary.
* To work effectively with you, your Secretary should:

  * Consult you on items for the agenda of committee meetings and allow you to control such meetings.
  * Keep you informed of correspondence received and forward invitations promptly.
  * Discuss matters pertaining to **District Grand Lodge** before a reply is sent.
  * Complete and return all required Annual Returns in consultation with you.
  * Alert you immediately if any problem arises.

#### The Director of Ceremonies

* Responsible for all matters of ritual and procedure.
* To ensure that you get the best possible support from your DC, he should undertake the following (though he may delegate, it nevertheless remains his responsibility):

  * Ensure that all Officers understand that they must be present at meetings.
  * Arrange deputies in cases where it is impossible for an officer to attend.
  * Ensure Candidates for Passing and Raising know their answers.
  * Check the Lodge layout before each meeting.
  * Finalise details of the Festive Board (wine taking, toasts, etc.) with you beforehand.
  * Ensure that the Junior Warden understands his role as Lodge Steward to ensure that visitors are catered for and properly looked after.
  * Take a keen interest in the progress being made in the Lodge of Instruction.

---

#### The Almoner

* Ensures that the Lodge meets its commitments to relieve poverty and distress.

* Duties include:

  * Liaising with the Masonic Charities.
  * Preparing petitions.
  * Keeping the Lodge informed of issues and coordinating the response.
  * Promptly report to you all cases of distress.
  * Be active in representing you to the wives and dependants of Lodge members.
  * Keep you informed of all developments in cases of distress.
  * Investigate and recommend solutions to situations involving financial difficulties.
  * Understand in detail the process for preparing a petition.

* The Almoner’s role calls for:

  * Energy.
  * Sensitivity.
  * Ability to deal easily with relative strangers.
  * Thorough understanding of Masonic Charities.
  * Mobility with ample time for visiting distressed members or their dependants.
  * The right fit — it is possibly more important to get the right man for the office of Almoner than most others.

---

#### The Charity Steward

* Key objective: that the Lodge enthusiastically and wholeheartedly supports Lodge charitable initiatives. Key to this will be the support you receive from your Charity Steward.
* To ensure this is understood, remind him to:

  * Send details of your charitable focus to all members early in the year.
  * Encourage the use of Gift Aid and be prompt in replying to requests for Gift Aid forms.
  * Energetically chase up those who are slow to respond.
  * Keep you informed of the progress being made.
  * Advise you promptly of correspondence received from the Masonic Charities.
  * Contribute his thoughts on the subject of additional fund-raising activities.

---

#### The Lodge Mentor

* Mentoring Scheme: provide a high level of personal support to ensure each member:

  * Enjoys being a Freemason.
  * Understands the principles of the Craft.
  * Becomes involved in his Lodge and all its activities as fully as possible.

> This is a whole-Lodge responsibility, but the Lodge Mentor coordinates.

The Lodge Mentor needs to ensure that a Mentor is appointed for every Candidate.

He must:

* Be fully aware of the Mentoring process, what it is trying to achieve, and what “success” looks like.
* Make sure that all members of his Lodge are aware of Mentoring and what benefits it brings.
* Ensure Personal Mentors understand the aims and objectives of the programme.
* Ensure Personal Mentors attend any training courses or workshops provided.
* Match the right Personal Mentor to the Candidate.
* Take time to ensure the Candidate and Personal Mentor form a good initial relationship.
* Complete a Detailed Lodge Mentoring Report and convey his thoughts to the District Mentor.
* Report his findings in Open Lodge.

---

#### Lodge Membership Officer

This relatively recent Office is a crucial one. It is his job to:

* Work closely with the Lodge Mentor and Almoner to create a membership group ensuring that members are fully aware of the Members’ Pathway and that the Lodge is appropriately engaged in promoting and embedding the concept.
* Ensure that recruitment and retention of members are given the highest priority at all times.
* Keep abreast of all District initiatives with regard to Membership issues and advise you accordingly.

---

### Most Importantly

> Try to make sure that Officers work as a **team**, in good heart, and with a sense of fun and fulfilment.

# PART TWO:  OCCUPYING THE CHAIR

There are three main sections in this part of the guidance:

* **Occupying the Chair for Regular meetings.**
* **Occupying the Chair for Installation Meetings.**
* **Occupying the Chair at Festive Boards.**

The module covers the key elements of Masonic occasions which require preparation by the Worshipful Master, and those which he must remember at the events themselves.

---

## Occupying the Chair for Regular meetings

Apart from the ceremony itself, there are a number of basic components which usually (though not in every case) comprise a regular meeting:

* **Summons** – the preparation and publication require close liaison with the Secretary, ensuring that you have your own working agenda close at hand during the meeting (with appropriate notes and reminders).
* **Processing into the Lodge** – welcoming visitors / VGO/Fraternal visits from other Lodges. (Your Lodge may not do this)
* **Eulogy** - to take place before any other agenda items.
* **Minutes** – proposed, seconded, voted, approved by Brethen, confirmed by your signature.
* **Salutations** – for Senior Brethren. (Your Lodge may not do this)
* **Ballots** – which should always be clearly understood by the Brethren regarding their purpose and how they will be conducted.
* **Election of officers** – WM, Treasurer, Tyler. (See Note 1) 
* **Reports** – Treasurer, Charity Steward, Almoner – sometimes Mentor or Membership Officer.
* **Alms** – collections, which should be clearly understood by the Brethren.
* **Risings**  
  These are formal communications during the meeting communicated as three rises:  
  1st Rising – Grand Lodge  
  2nd Rising – District Grand Lodge  
  3rd Rising – Your Lodge

---

## Occupying the Chair for Installation Meeting

* Part One of this module covered the need for proper preparation, particularly those years when occupying the Wardens’ chairs. Careful observation of Installation ceremonies is valuable preparation.
* In **DGLEA**, it is also customary for the **District DC** who will be attending the Installation to also attend any rehearsals or pre-meetings in order to familiarise himself and the members and the Lodge with all arrangements for the meeting itself. Ensure he is welcomed and make the most of this valuable opportunity.

There are, effectively, four main differences between a Regular Meeting and an Installation Meeting.

1. the introduction and entrance of a District Visitor.
2. Installation Ceremony itself.
3. the Appointment and Investiture of your Officers.
4. the Outgoing Procession.

The following section considers these in more detail.

---

### District Visitors

The Right Worshipful **District** Grand Master will wish to support the Installation by his own attendance but in view of the number of Lodges in the District, is more likely to send someone to represent him.  The basic procedures to be followed are as follows:

* Directly after the Minutes are confirmed, and after any internal Lodge business is completed and as early as possible, there will be a report at the door from a DC appointed by **District**, usually a **District Grand Steward**, to introduce the official visitor. You should address DC by his office and agree to receive ‘the Representative of the Right Worshipful District Grand Master’. Use the following form of words “We shall be pleased to receive him.”

There are two types of District visitor:

* **Representative of the RW PGM.**
* **One of the ‘Chain Gang’ – PGM / Dep PGM / Ass PGM’s**

#### Representative of the RW PGM.

* You will have been informed, previously, of his identity.
* He will be introduced and will ‘request’ admission. He does NOT have anyone officially attending with him and receives NO deputation, but other officers of Grand/**District** rank may be invited to escort him into the Lodge.
* He should be greeted and welcomed at your chair but NOT offered the Gavel.
* He is the first to receive salutations by name and rank as ‘the Representative of the RW PGM’. The **District** DC will conduct all Salutations.

#### One of the ‘Chain Gang’ – Provincial Grand Master, his Deputy or an Assistant Provincial Grand Master.

* He will be introduced by the **District** GDC (or his Deputy/Assts) and will **demand** admission.
* You should address the DC by his office and agree to receive him and he will process in behind any other members of the “Chains”, acting **District** Officers, Grand Officers etc.
* You should welcome him by his office (e.g. Right Worshipful **District** Grand Master) and **offer him the Gavel**.
* The **District** GDC will discharge the deputation and instruct the Brethren to be seated and he will then lead any necessary actions/salutations e.g. if the **District** Wardens are present to occupy the Wardens Chairs.
* The salutations are – **PGM (7) / DepPGM (5) / Ass PGM’s (5) / followed by his Warden (or acting Warden) (3) / followed by Grand Officers (3), followed by acting District Officers (3).**

**Actions to take**

* Work closely with the Secretary, Director of Ceremonies etc to ensure complete familiarity with names/ranks/ seating arrangements.
* Write down & practice the names & Offices & your welcome.
* Plan who will form the deputation.
* Know the Salutations.
* Include these details on your working agenda.

The Installation Ceremony is an important milestone for any Master Elect and for his Lodge. It can seem daunting to some, but it is always rewarding. It is important to try to relax and enjoy the occasion, particularly immediately after being placed in the Chair…a time to breathe and to collect oneself!

* The very first action is to invest the IPM – try to know these words well.
* Soon, the Brethren will be re-admitted and proclamations performed – this also gives you time to listen, to breathe, to arrange your paperwork and to check your next action.

#### Appointment & Investiture of your Officers – this is a very rewarding part of the ceremony and will flow very smoothly if you are well prepared.

* Make yourself a list of Officers, names, any key words plus any specific dialogue you may wish to use when you invest them.
* The DC will collect the collar and ask who you appoint. Use your list to announce the person and review your words while he is being escorted to you.
* **Brethren** will be escorted to & invested on your **right** side while **WBro’s** will be escorted to and invested to your **left**.

**Actions**

* Prepare your list of officers (including Mentor) with extra words.

#### Completing the Agenda

* Remember – Gavel given as ‘an emblem of power to enable you to preserve order in the Lodge’. Use it to bring order followed by ‘Brethren, next Item on the agenda is . . . .
* **Alms** – state where the proceeds will go and announce raffle at FB (if appropriate).
* **Risings**  
  These are formal communications during the meeting communicated as three rises:  
  1st Rising – Grand Lodge  
  2nd Rising – District Grand Lodge  
  3rd Rising – Your Lodge

#### Outgoing Procession - This will include your official visitors.

* **District DC** will call the Brethren to order. You are escorted to the head of the procession behind the Deacons, attended by your Wardens. The Wardens will be side-by-side for a Rep of RW PGM or one behind the other for a **District** Officer & his Warden.
* Wording is different for each: **Accompanied by ‘Rep of RW PGM’** or **Accompanying, the RW PGM** etc.
* Once in correct order the procession will move forward.
* Note: Find a convenient place outside the Lodge for everyone to congratulate you on your Installation.
* If photographs are to be taken, ensure that this is properly announced and undertaken as efficiently as possible.

## Occupying the Chair for The Festive Board (The Installation)

Clearly, every Masonic building is unique and the quality of experience to be enjoyed will always be dependent upon the surroundings, catering standards etc. Nevertheless, regardless of these factors, the Worshipful Master should always remember that he is expected to manage a host of things at the Festive Board. No doubt, the Director of Ceremonies and Secretary will have evolved a way of working so that nothing is forgotten but he should make himself acquainted with the running order.

Here are some useful tips for the incoming Master:

* The **District** GDC will generally take charge at the Installation FB. He will normally ask you for your plans and preferences (usually prior of the meeting) and will ensure everything runs smoothly.
* Be particularly mindful of time! Many will have travelled far and need to get home at a reasonable time! Get started early so you can fit everything in.
* This is your night – you are the star of the show. Enjoy it!

### Procession In

**District** GDC will announce your procession into the dining room. **Accompanied by ‘Rep of RW PGM’ OR Accompanying, the RW PGM (or other)** etc, followed by the **District** GDC to rapturous applause.

* **District DC** will gavel to bring Brethren to order. He will wait for SW / JW Gavels (and Silence) before speaking.
* Grace before dining then **District** GDC will announce ‘Be seated’.

### Toasts – Remember Grace before the toasts! Often sung in many lodges.

* Ensure you have an up to date Toast List from the Secretary – suggest generating your own working copy. Useful for **District** GDC to have a copy of your notes.
* Know who is proposing & responding (if appropriate) to each toast – especially toast to visitors and the Reply. **District** GDC will ask you who is proposing the toasts.
* **District** GDC will gavel and announce the proposer and any responder for the first five toasts up to and including the representative of PGM/response. Then he will usually hand over to the Lodge DC.
* When it is your turn, be strong with the Gavel & wait for SW / JW and Silence before speaking.
* Toasts should be specific and focused – encourage proposers to stick to the point & purpose of the toast!
* Recognised pre-amble (as appropriate) : WM / RW PGM (DepPGM, APGM, Rep of RW PGM) / Bro Warden(s) / Brethren All. It is my pleasure to propose the next toast / The next toast is . . / I give you the next toast . . /
* State the toast clearly & call Brethren to order (i.e. To order Brethren / Brethren be upstanding – the toast is . . . / Rise and drink a toast to . . )
* If the Festive board is honoured with the presence of one of the dignitaries on the toast list – make the toast personal with some welcoming words. This will help generate a warm response from the reply. After the toast and Fire, remember to claim a response from the dignitary!

### Firing

* The **District** DC may request that firing should not take place in the presence of the RW **District** Grand Master and his ‘Team’ or Deputation.
* Toasts to ‘The King’ must not be fired. ‘The King & the Craft’ and every other toast may be fired. Fire is usually given by the proposer – never sprung on an unsuspecting individual!
* Use a recognised pre-amble for 1st fire: **Fire Brethren, taking the time from me.** Subsequent Fire: **To order Brethren.** Fire should be slow and distinguished!

### Wine Taking – **District** GDC will ask you for a Wine Taking list. (If this is normally practised in your Lodge). Be prepared for him to adjust the list!!

* Make sure recipients have wine (or a drink) to receive the toast!
* Try to limit the wine takings.

### Raffle

* Remember to solicit your members for raffle prizes ahead of the meeting to give a good selection. This will enhance ticket sales.
* Gavel and wait for SW / JW & Silence before announcing “Stewards will be circulating with Raffle tickets.”
* Re-announce the cause – remember a good cause which is well explained will be better received and likely to generate higher ticket sales. Urge Brethren to give generously!
* Raffle draw is generally done before the toast to visitors, but **District** GDC may well fit it in during a quiet period.

### Response to WM Toast

This is your time to address the Brethren! They want to hear what you have to say! **Caution:** Be prepared – the Master’s song can be very emotional! We have seen many a new Master choked up so much he is unable to speak – even to the point of tears!

* **Stand Up!** Stand tall and straight – shows that you are proud and want to set an example!
* **Look Up!** Let Brethren see your face. Also helps to fill your lungs and project your voice!
* **Speak Up!** Prepare your words beforehand. Use bullet points! Use this opportunity to share your social plans for the year (i.e. functions or Ladies Festival).
* Some Lodges also share details of the Alms collection and Raffle takings.

### Toast to Visitors

Select a suitable Proposer and Visitor to reply and inform them as early as possible.

* Toast is to the Visitors – make it TO the Visitors. Make it sincere! Make it enjoyable!
* Some Lodges use poems, phrases, stories or jokes! Main point is never to use any material that may cause offence!
* Often nice for the proposer to give Fire for this toast – make sure **District** GDC knows!
* After the toast and Fire, remember to claim a response from the Visitor who will reply.
* The selected Visitor should be informed of any Lodge traditions he should follow or comply with. He should also embrace the same guidelines regarding stories or jokes.

### Tyler’s Toast – **Catch the eye of the Tyler** before this toast to make sure he is prepared.

Summoned by a Double-knock! Ensure Tyler is well versed with both the wording and Fire technique.

**Actions**

* Practice use of the Gavel – loud and distinct
* Generate your own working plan of the Festive board including Wine Takings, Absent Brethren , Pre-amble and Toast List with proposers / replies.
* Select a worthy cause for the Raffle and promote accordingly
* Prepare your response to the Master’s Toast – practice it!

## Occupying the Chair for Festive Board (Regular Meeting)

* The Festive Board for a regular meeting follows a similar format to that of the Installation meeting but slightly less formal and more relaxed.
* Effectively the Lodge DC replaces the **District** GDC and will take charge at the regular FB. He too is likely to ask for your plans and preferences and is responsible for ensuring everything runs smoothly

**Toasts – Remember Grace!**

* Know who is proposing & responding (if appropriate) to each toast – especially toast to visitors.
* Be strong with the Gavel & wait for SW / JW and Silence before speaking. The Lodge DC will announce the proposer for each toast.
* Use the recognised pre-amble (as appropriate) : WM / (Rep of RW PGM) / Bro Warden(s) / Brethren All.
* Fire is given by Lodge DC (or the proposer)
* If the Festive board is honoured with the official visit from a **District “Chain”**– make the toast personal with some welcoming words. This will help generate a warm response from the reply. After the toast and fire, remember to claim a response from him.

**Wine Taking** – Lodge DC will ask you for a Wine Taking list. (If normal practise in your Lodge)

* Make sure recipients have wine (or a drink) to receive the toast!
* Not so important to keep wine taking to only 4 – 6 only but be sensible.
* Order – Rep of RW PGM (VGO) / Wardens, Grand Officers, **District** Grand Officers, Initiate.
* Aim to get wine takings completed between courses.

**Response to WM Toast – Remember!**

* **Stand Up! Look Up! Speak Up!**
* Try to bring some new information to each festive board to keep Brethren interested.

**Toast to Visitors** – Suitable Proposer and Visitor to reply and inform them as early as possible.

**Actions**

* Practice use of the Gavel – loud and distinct.
* Update your own working plan of the Festive board including Wine Takings, Absent Brethren, Pre-amble and Toast List with proposers / replies.
* Select a worthy cause for the Raffle and promote accordingly.
* Prepare your response to the Master’s Toast – practice it!
* Plan a short-list of Proposers for the Visitors Toast.

✅ **Note 1: Election or Appointment of a Non-Paid Tyler**

According to the *Book of Constitutions – Craft Rules of the United Grand Lodge of England (September 2025 edition)*, the rule governing the election and appointment of the Tyler is **Rule 113**, which provides:

> **Rule 113. Tyler of a Lodge**
> “The Tyler shall be elected by the members on the regular day of election of the Master.
> A Lodge, however, may resolve that a subscribing member of the Lodge shall be Tyler without emolument, in which case he shall be appointed with the other Officers by the Master.” 

**Interpretation for Lodge practice (DGLEA):**

* The **default position** is that the Tyler is **elected annually** by the Lodge at the same meeting as the Master and Treasurer.
* However, if the Tyler is **not paid (i.e., without emolument)** and is a **subscribing member** of the Lodge, the Lodge may pass a resolution to adopt this arrangement.
* Once that resolution is in force, the **non-paid subscribing Tyler** is **appointed by the Master** together with the other officers, rather than being elected.

**In summary:**

> When the Tyler is a subscribing member and serves without pay, he is appointed (not elected) by the Worshipful Master in accordance with *Rule 113* of the Book of Constitutions.

*(Source: Book of Constitutions – Craft Rules § 113, p. 57, UGLE Sept 2025 edition.)*
