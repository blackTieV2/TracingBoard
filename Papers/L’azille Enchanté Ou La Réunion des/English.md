## The Enchanted Azille or The Reunion of the Two Sexes Apprentice Grade 

### Chamber of Reflection

This chamber must be adorned in white with a table in the middle, covered with a white cloth, two armchairs, one in front of the table and the other beside it. The Initiate is seated within without a chair, facing the table. The second armchair is used on days when the illustrious Master or Warden is present there. The table must include a vanity mirror and a writing desk with a single lit lamp in the said chamber.

---

### Preparation of the Initiate

The Sister Deputy of the Very Illustrious Master examines and asks the Lady or Young Woman if she harbors any critical doubts, such as pregnancy or other conditions, and verifies whether she is in a state to endure the trials or requires alternate accommodations. She ensures that the attire is proper and agreeable to behold. The Sister Deputy removes any decorations or ornaments causing discomfort and places a blue ribbon upon her, presenting the hands of the Initiate to the V.I.M. as a sign of submission. She observes the candidate in question to confirm her readiness to proceed. The V.I.M. instructs the Deputy to prepare the Initiate for admission.

The Sister then examines the hands of the Initiate, removes any grandeur of rings, and places her beside the blue ribbon that awaits her. A distinct veil is placed upon her, with the remark that the innocent are always entitled to justice. This symbolizes the truth she must later demonstrate and reveals the right angles that Freemasons hold under the guidance of truth. She uncovers the left shoulder and blindfolds her with a white handkerchief, requiring her to develop a firm and blind faith, and to act accordingly.

She is then introduced into the ceremony by five knocks.

---

### Decoration of the Lodge

The East of the Lodge must feature a throne at the back of which must be a rising sun and three columns, all in silver and garnet colors, with seven stars of the same hue. The throne must be elevated by five steps and well-lit. The dais or throne must be supported by six white-enamelled columns, evenly spaced. Around the dais or throne is a small round table covered with a fiery-colored cloth edged with silver fringe.

The Grand Inspector and the Grand Inspectress face the West. The Grand Inspectress adds her presence by entering under the platform, while the Grand Inspector sits near the door of the West to maintain supervision.

The Lodge must be draped in white with medallions at intervals formed by garlands of flowers supported by flying cherubs. These medallions must represent one of the Lodge's attributes, beautifully illuminated. The ceiling must be adorned with a crystal chandelier carried by garlands of flowers intertwined to give the impression that a single cherub supports them all with its right hand. These chandeliers must have five branches, each bearing five candles placed in the four corners of the Lodge and one in the center at the room’s end. The four chandeliers must illuminate the throne.

The tableau must have on its table a small image adorned with a tapestry of cherubs. It bears a blue ribbon. Next to it hangs a trellis. The tableau must be bordered in blue.

### Opening of the Lodge

The Very Illustrious addresses the Senior Grand Inspector and asks:  
**Q.** What is the first duty of a mason and masoness?  
**A.** The Illustrious declares that we are protected from the profane.  

The Grand Inspector confirms:  
**Q.** Grand Inspector, ensure the Lodge and the G.J. are secure, ensuring that the Lodge is covered and that we are safe.  
The Grand Inspector replies that the Lodge is well-covered and that everyone is ready to receive the Masters.  

**Q.** My sister Grand Inspectress, what is the first duty of a mason and masoness?  
**A.** To obey, to work, and to remain silent.  

The Illustrious orders that the Brethren and Sisters clap their hands together five times, saying "Vivat" each time.  
The Illustrious strikes five times while facing the table and says: "And respect to the G.J. and the G.I.".  
The Illustrious instructs everyone to proclaim: "To work".  
The Lodge is open.

### Discourse

O God, Lord, all Masons constitute one single and Holy Despot, begotten, unto whom they began to gain knowledge, and with whom they were united. Cain, who succeeded Him in the great office.

The Master, having fixed his gaze upon the Western region, was marked by the Divine through the founding of a city, which He named Enoch, after His son. Thereafter, Jubal—one of his descendants—came to know the Divine Name and, being a shepherd, established lodges. Yet the most renowned mason of all was Enoch, the son of Ygrand, who journeyed to ensure your removal from the common realm; thus humanity has been reestablished in its earthly parish.

Blessed with a spirit of prosperity, he recognized that God’s wrath would not delay its manifestation by a universal flood. Fearing that women—his subordinates—might be exposed to it, he proceeded to inscribe great precepts upon pillars, on which he engraved the principles and the Rules. He made one Column of stone and the other of brick, so that if the flood should come and destroy the latter, the stone Column would remain to preserve for posterity the memory of all that was sacred therein.

Foresight achieved its purpose, for it is said that this very column still existed in the age of the Emperor Vespasian. Its summit was presided over by three judges: the Lady, the Divine Judge, and the Worker of Nature. Yet those who refused consent, having gone forth to commit crimes of every sort, provoked God to intensify His wrath, and it was fire that found clemency before Him. God forewarned that He would flood the earth, and commanded, to sustain them, the building of an Ark of five levels, whose shape and proportions Rain itself indicated. It was made of a fire-resistant bean-wood, already well seasoned.

Five days before the Flood, once the Ark was built, Noah entered it with his family, and by acting as one in unity, they preserved all things, including the instructions and the secrets imparted by God, and most especially those of Masonry.

When Noah and his family departed from the Ark, they and their descendants dwelt for a long time upon the heights of the mountains, fearing another inundation. In due course, realizing that it was possible to settle upon the plain, they established various communities there. Until then, all humankind shared one common tongue; yet there arose some who rebelled against the will of God and, in their pride, turned to masonry to erect a Tower and protect themselves from such a catastrophe. They threatened the earth with a second flood and raised, for this purpose, a Tower they intended to reach the heavens—a bold undertaking indeed. But God, as everyone knows, confounded their languages, forcing them to abandon the work and scatter across the lands.
